How to play

Goal

Fill the grid so each row and each column contains every number once (1 to 4 on a 4×4 grid, 1 to 6 on 6×6), and every inequality sign is true.

How it works

  • The signs between squares say which is bigger. < and > work across; ∧ means the square below is bigger and ∨ means the square above is bigger. The sign always points at the smaller number.
  • Darker numbers are given. Tap an empty square, then tap a number. ⌫ clears it.
  • Repeated numbers turn red, and so does a sign that is broken.
  • Every puzzle has exactly one solution.

Tips

  • A square that is smaller than its neighbour can never hold the largest number; a square that is bigger can never hold 1.
  • Follow chains: if a < b < c across three squares on a 4×4 grid, then a can only be 1 or 2.
  • When a row has just one gap, it's the number that's missing.

A little history

Futoshiki means “inequality” in Japanese. The puzzle was created in Japan in 2001 by Tamaki Seto and became a newspaper favourite worldwide after Sudoku.
