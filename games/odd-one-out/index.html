How to play

Goal

Find the one square whose colour is a little different from all the others. Keep going as the difference gets smaller.

How it works

  • All the squares are almost the same shade, but one is slightly lighter or darker. Tap it.
  • Each correct tap takes you up a level: the grid grows and the difference shrinks.
  • You have three tries. A wrong tap costs a try, and the right square is shown.
  • There is no clock. Look as long as you like.

Tips

  • Soften your gaze and look at the whole grid at once instead of one square at a time.
  • Squint a little: small differences in shade show up more clearly.
  • Turn up your screen brightness. Very dim screens hide small differences.

The history of Odd One Out

Seeing small differences in colour is a real skill that doctors and designers test. The Japanese ophthalmologist Shinobu Ishihara published his colour-vision plates in 1917, and in 1943 the American Dean Farnsworth devised the 100 Hue Test, in which people arrange coloured caps by shade. Spot-the-difference puzzles like this one are a gentle, everyday cousin of those tests.
